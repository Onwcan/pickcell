How far the tool travels after the safety runtime decides
=========================================================

Axis limits: 2.0 m/s, 8 m/s^2, 40 m/s^3.

Reaction travel is the distance covered before the cell has reacted --
linear in speed and in the reaction time. Braking is what the arm covers
decelerating under those limits, which motionkit computes and which grows
faster than linearly. A guard is positioned from the sum.

The penalty runs the other way to the instinct
---------------------------------------------
Swapping shared memory for a 200 ms poll multiplies the guard distance
by 3.4x at 0.25 m/s and by only 1.8x at 2.00 m/s.

The absolute cost is worse on a fast cell -- 380 mm against 47 mm -- and
the relative cost is worse on a slow one, because braking distance grows
faster than linearly with speed while reaction travel does not. A slow
machine has almost no braking distance to hide a slow link behind. "We
run slowly here, so latency does not matter" has it backwards.


What this says
--------------
The poll interval is not only a latency. It is floor space, and it is
cycle time. Every millisecond of reaction costs the tool's speed in
millimetres of clearance, and a cell that cannot have the clearance pays
for it by running slower instead.

The braking term is the floor. No link, however fast, gets below it --
which is the honest limit on what this repository's measurement can buy.
What the measurement decides is how much is added on top.

Reaction figures are worst cases, from evidence/reaction-vs-poll-interval.txt.
A guard is positioned for the slowest reaction the system can have, and
for a poll that is not a measurement at all: it is the poll interval.
