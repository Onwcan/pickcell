Losing the safety runtime must stop the cell
============================================

Captured from the docker compose stack: safeedge on :9100, pickcelld on :9200,
polling /readyz every 100 ms.


BEFORE -- the cell kept moving on a permit nobody had reissued
--------------------------------------------------------------

  $ curl -s localhost:9200/metrics          # safety runtime UP
  pickcell_cycles_completed 5
  pickcell_held_by_safety 0

  $ docker compose stop safety-runtime
  $ sleep 6 && curl -s localhost:9200/metrics
  pickcell_cycles_completed 7               <-- advanced by two
  pickcell_held_by_safety 0                 <-- still not held
  pickcell_safety_poll_failures 2

The safety runtime was gone and every poll was failing, and the cell completed
two more pick-and-place cycles. HttpPollSafetyLink::poll() re-stamped
observed_monotonic_ns on every call, so a cached "torque permitted" looked
freshly confirmed forever. Absence of information read as permission.


AFTER -- a permit expires
-------------------------

  $ curl -s localhost:9200/metrics          # safety runtime UP
  pickcell_cycles_completed 2
  pickcell_held_by_safety 0
  pickcell_cycles_on_stale_safety 0

  $ docker compose stop safety-runtime
  $ sleep 8 && curl -s localhost:9200/metrics
  pickcell_cycles_completed 2               <-- frozen
  pickcell_held_by_safety 1
  pickcell_cycles_on_stale_safety 7656
  pickcell_safety_poll_failures 3

  $ sleep 6 && curl -s localhost:9200/metrics
  pickcell_cycles_completed 2               <-- still frozen
  pickcell_held_by_safety 1


THE FIX

  * The link reports when its information was OBTAINED, not when it was asked
    for, so a cached value visibly ages.
  * The cell holds once that age exceeds Cell::Config::max_safety_age_ns
    (250 ms by default, against a 100 ms poll).
  * cycles_on_stale_safety is counted separately from cycles_without_safety:
    "never heard from the runtime" and "heard, but too long ago to believe" are
    different faults with different causes.

  CellSafety.StopsBelievingAPermitThatHasGoneStale is the regression test.


WHAT IS STILL NOT FIXED

  SharedMemorySafetyLink has the same exposure and this does not close it. If
  the writer dies, the seqlock keeps its last value indefinitely and the reader
  cannot tell a live writer from a dead one -- the read succeeds either way.

  Closing it needs the writer to heartbeat, which is exactly what the sequence
  number in safeedge's safety telegram is for. Recorded here rather than
  quietly left out.
